Liquid Density Tower

Grades 3–5Grades 6–81 Hour

Stack kitchen liquids in colorful layers that don't mix.

Question: Why do some liquids float on top of others?
Hypothesis: If I pour liquids slowly into a glass, then they will stack in order from heaviest (most dense) at the bottom to lightest on top.
Safety: Rubbing alcohol is flammable and poisonous: adults only, keep away from flames, or skip it.

Supplies (mostly already in your kitchen)

  • Honey
  • Dish soap
  • Water (with food coloring)
  • Vegetable oil
  • Rubbing alcohol (optional, adult only)
  • A tall clear glass
  • Small objects to drop in: grape, cherry tomato, paper clip, cork

Steps

  1. Pour honey into the bottom of the glass.
  2. Slowly pour dish soap down the side of the glass, then colored water, then oil.
  3. Wait a minute for the layers to settle.
  4. Gently drop in small objects and see which layer each one stops at.
  5. Draw and label the layers.

The science, explained

Density is how much stuff (mass) is packed into a space (volume). Denser liquids sink below less dense ones. An object stops at the layer where it's denser than the liquid above but less dense than the liquid below.

Common question

Board tips

  • Show a labeled diagram of the layers.
  • Make a table of where each object floated.
  • Define density in one sentence.
