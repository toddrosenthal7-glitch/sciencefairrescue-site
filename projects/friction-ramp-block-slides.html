How Steep Before a Block Slides?

Grades 9–121 Hour

How steep can a ramp get before a block slides? You measure it yourself: tilt a covered board slowly, bracket the first sliding angle for paper, plastic and cloth, and use μs = tan(θ) to estimate an effective static-friction coefficient for each covering. This video shows you how to run that test and handle the uncertainty. It doesn't give you an answer.

This is an illustrated instructional guide with synthetic narration, not filmed experimental evidence. The setup has not been physically tested, and no test results are claimed. You run the test and record your own observations.

Question: How Steep Before a Block Slides?
Hypothesis: Write your own If–then–because prediction before testing; this guide doesn't give you an answer.
Safety: Have a teacher help with calibration and safety. Keep the apparatus low, use a light, broad block, put a soft catch at the low end and keep hands clear of the block's path. No student sawing, power cutting, glass, heavy added weights or tall stacks. Never tilt past 60°.

Supplies (mostly already in your kitchen)

  • Wooden craft blocks (about 40 x 40 x 10 mm)
  • Thin craft plywood board (about 300 x 150 mm, 6–10 mm thick)
  • Protractor, about 120 mm, 1° marks
  • Clear plastic document folders (smooth, non-adhesive)
  • Thin tightly woven cotton fabric
  • Thread and small washers (plumb bob)
  • Masking tape
  • Non-slip shelf liner (for the low-end stop)
  • Scientific calculator
  • Tri-fold display board, 36x48
  • printer paper, a ruler, a pencil, a towel for the soft catch, a record sheet and a helper. A side-on camera is optional.
Time: Setup 25–40 min, 30 bracketed trials 40–65 min, calculations and write-up 35–50 min (100–155 min total). Advanced uncertainty work can add time. These are unvalidated planning estimates, not measured times. The video is only instruction. Check your teacher's requirements.

Steps

  1. Fix the low end of the board against a non-slip stop, work low and away from the table edge, and put a soft catch past the low end.
  2. Wrap one covering flat and secure it underneath, with no tape or seams in the block's path. Mark a start line 200 mm up the board.
  3. Mount the protractor upright on a raised tab beside the ramp, hang a thread and bob from its centre, and check the zero with the board flat.
  4. Place the block at the start line, then raise the board in about 1° steps, waiting about 2 seconds each time. Record the last no-slide and first sliding angle.
  5. Run 10 valid trials per covering in the blocked order shown. Stop at 60°; record "no slip at 60" if it never slides.
  6. Convert each midpoint angle with μ = tan(θc), report the mean and sample SD, and state an angular uncertainty bound.
