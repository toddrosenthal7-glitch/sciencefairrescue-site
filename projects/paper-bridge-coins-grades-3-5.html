Can a Paper Bridge Hold Coins?

Grades 3–51 Hour

This is an illustrated instructional guide with synthetic narration, not filmed experimental evidence. The setup has not been physically tested, and no test results are claimed. You run the test and record your own observations.

Question: Can a Paper Bridge Hold Coins?
Hypothesis: Write your own If–then–because prediction before testing; this guide doesn't give you an answer.
Safety: Work with an adult the whole time. Keep coins away from small children and pets (choking hazard). Work away from the table edge and keep the books low and steady. Never push down on the bridge. No glass or heavy weights. Always stop at 30 coins, and stop if anything becomes unsafe.

Supplies (mostly already in your kitchen)

  • Printer paper (one pack, all the same size)
  • Lightweight paper cups (flat base)
  • Ruler
  • Shallow tray to catch coins
  • Timer or clock
  • Tri-fold display board, 36x48
  • 2 equal stacks of books (3–5 cm tall), a pencil, and 30 coins of the same kind.
Time: Building, testing and writing take longer than this 6-minute video. No times are given or claimed. This video is a guide, not a finished science fair project. Check your teacher's requirements.

Steps

  1. With your adult, set two level book stacks (3–5 cm tall) 15 cm apart over a tray.
  2. Lay the long side of the paper across the gap with equal overlap on each stack.
  3. Make three shapes from matching sheets: A flat, B doubled lengthwise, C folded like a fan and opened to 10 cm wide.
  4. Make three fresh bridges of each shape (9 total) and label them A1–C3. Never reuse tested paper.
  5. Put the empty cup in the middle, wait 5 seconds, then add coins one at a time, waiting 5 seconds after each.
  6. Stop when the bridge fails or at 30 coins. Write "at least 30" if it still holds.
  7. Test in the worksheet order, then compare the three counts for each shape.
