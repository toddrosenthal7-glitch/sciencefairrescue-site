Dancing Raisins: Soda vs. Water

Grades K–25 Minutes

Raisins float up and sink down in soda. Done in five minutes.

Question: Do raisins dance in fizzy soda but not in plain water?
Hypothesis: If I drop raisins in clear soda, then they will rise and sink, because gas bubbles stick to them and lift them.
Safety: Ask an adult before using soda. Don't drink the experiment.

Supplies (mostly already in your kitchen)

  • Raisins
  • Clear soda (like lemon-lime) or sparkling water
  • Plain tap water
  • 2 clear glasses

Steps

  1. Fill one glass with soda and one with tap water.
  2. Drop 5 raisins into each glass.
  3. Watch for 3 minutes and count how many times the raisins rise to the top in each glass.
  4. Write down what you see.

The science, explained

Soda has carbon dioxide gas dissolved in it. Bubbles grab onto the wrinkly raisins and make them float. At the top the bubbles pop, so the raisins sink and start again. Plain water has no gas, so the raisins just sit on the bottom.

Board tips

  • Draw a picture of a raisin with bubbles stuck to it.
  • Show a tally chart of rises in soda vs. water.
