Baking Soda Volcano: Which Mix Erupts Biggest?

Grades K–2Grades 3–51 Hour

The classic, turned into a real experiment by changing how much vinegar you use.

Question: Does more vinegar make a bigger eruption?
Hypothesis: If I add more vinegar to the same amount of baking soda, then the foam will rise higher, because more acid can react with the baking soda.
Safety: Wear safety glasses. Do not taste anything, and do it on a tray or outside.

Supplies (mostly already in your kitchen)

  • Baking soda
  • White vinegar
  • Dish soap
  • Food coloring (optional)
  • A plastic cup or small bottle
  • Measuring spoons and cups
  • A baking tray to catch the mess
  • Ruler

Steps

  1. Put the cup on the tray.
  2. Add 1 tablespoon of baking soda and a squirt of dish soap.
  3. Pour in 1/4 cup of vinegar and measure how high the foam rises with the ruler.
  4. Clean out the cup and repeat with 1/2 cup, then 3/4 cup of vinegar.
  5. Do each amount 3 times and write down every result.

The science, explained

Vinegar (an acid) reacts with baking soda (a base) and makes carbon dioxide gas. The dish soap traps the gas in bubbles, so you get foam. Once all the baking soda is used up, adding more vinegar can't make more gas.

Common question

Board tips

  • Make a bar chart: vinegar amount vs. foam height.
  • Take a photo of each eruption at its highest point.
  • Explain why the foam stopped growing at some point (the baking soda ran out).
