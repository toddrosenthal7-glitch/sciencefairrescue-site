The Naked Egg: Vinegar Dissolves the Shell

Grades 3–5Grades 6–8Overnight

Soak an egg in vinegar and the shell disappears, leaving a bouncy egg.

Question: What happens to an eggshell left in vinegar?
Hypothesis: If I soak an egg in vinegar, then the shell will dissolve, because the acid in vinegar reacts with the calcium carbonate in the shell.
Safety: Raw egg: wash your hands after. Shine a flashlight through it, but don't bounce it high!

Supplies (mostly already in your kitchen)

  • 1 raw egg
  • White vinegar
  • A clear jar or glass
  • A spoon

Steps

  1. Put the egg in the jar and cover it with vinegar.
  2. Watch for tiny bubbles on the shell in the first few minutes.
  3. Leave it 24 to 48 hours. Change the vinegar after the first day if you can.
  4. Gently rinse the egg with water and rub off any leftover shell.
  5. Measure around the egg before and after with a string.

The science, explained

Eggshells are mostly calcium carbonate. Vinegar's acetic acid breaks it down and makes carbon dioxide (the bubbles). The thin membrane under the shell stays, so the egg holds its shape. Water also soaks in through the membrane, so the egg gets a little bigger.
