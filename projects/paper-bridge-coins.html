Which Paper Bridge Holds More Coins?

Grades 6–81 Hour

Which paper bridge holds more coins? You find out by testing it yourself: fold a flat, a doubled and an accordion bridge from the same paper, load each one with identical coins under the same conditions, and compare all nine trials. This video shows you how to run that fair test. It doesn't give you an answer.

Watch on YouTube: Which Paper Bridge Holds More Coins? | Build & Test 3 Designs Yourself

This is an illustrated instructional guide with synthetic narration, not filmed experimental evidence. The setup has not been physically tested, and no test results or winning design are claimed. You run the test and record your own observations.

Question: Which Paper Bridge Holds More Coins?
Hypothesis: Write your own If–then–because prediction before testing; this guide doesn't give you an answer.
Safety: Have an adult check the setup. Work on a clear table away from the edge. Keep hands and faces out of the collapse area. Coins are a choking hazard: keep younger children and pets away. Stop at 30 coins or if anything is unstable. Never sit, stand or press on the bridge. No heavy weights or glass.

Supplies (mostly already in your kitchen)

  • Printer paper (one pack, all the same size)
  • Lightweight paper cups (flat base)
  • Ruler
  • Shallow tray to catch coins
  • Timer
  • Tri-fold display board, 36x48
  • 2 equal stacks of books (3–5 cm tall), a pencil, and 30 coins of one kind.
Time: Setup 5–10 min, nine tests 20–35 min, analysis 20–30 min. These are planning estimates, not measured times. This 5-minute video is a guide, not a finished science fair project. Check your teacher's requirements.

Steps

  1. Set two low, level book stacks 15 cm apart over a tray.
  2. Fold three designs from matching sheets: A flat, B doubled lengthwise, C accordion.
  3. Make three fresh sheets of each design (9 total). Never reuse tested paper.
  4. Center the empty cup on the bridge, then add coins one at a time in the same order, waiting 5 seconds after each.
  5. Stop at failure or at 30 coins. Record capped tests as "at least 30."
  6. Repeat for all nine trials, find the average per design, and graph your results.
