Lemon Battery: How Many Lemons to Light an LED?

Grades 6–81 Hour

Turn lemons into a battery and test how many it takes to light a small LED.

Question: How many lemons in a row does it take to light an LED?
Hypothesis: If I connect more lemons in a chain, then the voltage will go up, because each lemon cell adds its voltage to the others.
Safety: Have an adult help push in the nails. Don't eat the lemons afterward.

Supplies (mostly already in your kitchen)

  • 4 lemons
  • 4 copper coins or copper strips
  • 4 galvanized (zinc-coated) nails
  • Alligator clip wires
  • A small LED
  • A multimeter (optional but great)

Steps

  1. Roll each lemon on the table to get the juice flowing.
  2. Push one nail and one copper coin into each lemon, about 2 cm apart.
  3. Clip the copper of lemon 1 to the nail of lemon 2, and keep going down the chain.
  4. Connect the free copper and free nail at the ends to the LED (try flipping it if it doesn't light).
  5. If you have a multimeter, measure the voltage with 1, 2, 3, and 4 lemons.

The science, explained

The zinc and copper are two different metals, and the lemon juice is an acid that lets charged particles move between them. That makes a small electric current. One lemon gives less than 1 volt, and most LEDs need more, so connecting lemons in series adds the voltages together.

Board tips

  • Draw a circuit diagram of your lemon chain.
  • Graph number of lemons vs. voltage.
  • Note whether the LED glowed and how bright.
