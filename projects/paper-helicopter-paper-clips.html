Do More Paper Clips Make a Paper Helicopter Fall Faster?

Grades 6–81 Hour

Do more paper clips make a paper helicopter fall faster? You find out by testing it yourself: build two matching paper helicopters, time 30 drops from a fixed 1.5 m height with one, two and three clips, and compare the mean fall times and their spread. This video shows you how to run that controlled test. It doesn't give you an answer.

This is an illustrated instructional guide with synthetic narration, not filmed experimental evidence. The setup has not been physically tested, and no test results are claimed. You run the test and record your own observations.

Question: Do More Paper Clips Make a Paper Helicopter Fall Faster?
Hypothesis: Write your own If–then–because prediction before testing; this guide doesn't give you an answer.
Safety: An adult supervises scissors. Paper clips are small parts: keep them away from faces, pets and younger children. Stay on the floor: no chairs, tables, stairs or climbing. Work indoors with fans off and a clear landing area. No outdoor wind or road testing. Stop if clips can't be secured.

Supplies (mostly already in your kitchen)

  • Copier paper, 75–90 g/m²
  • Standard steel paper clips (uncoated, about 28–32 mm)
  • Metric ruler
  • Scissors (adult supervision)
  • Tape measure
  • Digital stopwatch
  • Index cards (for order cards 1, 2, 3)
  • Masking tape (height marker only)
  • Tri-fold display board, 36x48
  • a pencil, a notebook, and a helper to time. A camera is optional.
Time: Build 20–30 min, hand-timed trials 25–40 min, graph and write-up 20–30 min (65–100 min total). Frame analysis may add 20–45 min. These are unvalidated planning estimates, not measured times. Video runtime is not experiment time. Check your teacher's requirements.

Steps

  1. Cut two 60 x 180 mm rectangles from the same sheet and label them H1 and H2.
  2. Cut and fold each into a helicopter: two 30 x 90 mm blades folded in opposite directions, a 20 mm stem, and the bottom 10 mm folded up.
  3. Nest one, two or three clips at the same spot on the bottom of the stem. Check the fit before every drop.
  4. Mark 1.50 m above the floor (or 1.20 m for the whole experiment if you can't reach it with feet on the floor).
  5. Shuffle order cards 1, 2, 3 for each round. Run five rounds per helicopter: 30 valid drops in total.
  6. Release without pushing or spin. The helper times from release to first floor contact, to 0.1 s.
  7. Record every drop, including invalid ones. Find the mean, minimum and maximum for each helicopter and clip count, then graph clip count vs mean fall time.
