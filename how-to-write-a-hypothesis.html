How to Write a Science Fair Hypothesis

Pattern: If I change ___, then ___ will ___, because ___.

Independent variable

The one thing you change on purpose.

Dependent variable

What you measure.

Controlled variables

Everything you keep the same.

Examples

  • If I add more paper clips to a paper helicopter, then it will fall faster, because the extra weight pulls it down harder than the spinning blades can slow it. (project)
  • If I fold the paper like a fan, then it will hold more coins than flat paper, because the folds make the paper harder to bend. (project)
  • If I sprinkle salt on an ice cube, then it will melt faster than an ice cube with sugar or sand, because salt lowers the temperature at which ice can stay frozen. (project)

5-point checklist

  • Can you test it with the supplies you have?
  • Does it name one thing you change?
  • Does it name one thing you measure?
  • Does it say which way you expect the result to go?
  • Does it give a reason?

Common questions

How do you write a science fair hypothesis?

Use the If, then, because pattern: If I change the independent variable, then the dependent variable will increase, decrease or change, because of a reason from your research.

What is the difference between independent and dependent variables?

The independent variable is the one thing you change on purpose. The dependent variable is what you measure. Controlled variables are everything you keep the same.

What if my hypothesis is wrong?

That is fine. Say your results did not support your hypothesis, explain why, and say what you would test next. Scientists say supported or not supported, not proven.
